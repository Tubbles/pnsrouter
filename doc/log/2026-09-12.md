# 2026-09-12

## Arcs: the hold is lifted

Tags: #decision #plan #upstream #arcs

- The user lifted the arc hold of 2026-09-10 and asked for the forum and the tracker to be searched for design and constraints first. Findings, all with the pointers in `doc/reference/kicad/09-arcs.md` sections 7 and 9 and in this entry:
  - Forum: the only design discussion is discourse t/51 (2018, revived 2024). The maintainer wants "only arcs, not splines or so, because arcs can easily be exported to Gerber", and set one constraint: curved traces must not break the push and shove router ("it would be very bad to have curved traces, but no longer be able to use the push&shove router"). In 2024 he rated rounded traces as irrelevant for "probably 99.99% of all PCB designs". t/13 (the router thread) never mentions arcs.
  - Tracker: LibrePCB#1710 (March 2026, open, "low prio", "file format") asks for curved traces for RF as a step in the trace bend cycle. #354 settled the polygon arc gesture, #1263 (open) asks for a fillet tool on outlines. #1537 carries one inaccurate contributor remark about bezier curves. Gerber arcs were settled in 2019 (#247, #504) and STEP outline arcs in PR #1484. PR #1770 (April 2026) added arc drawing to the board polygon and zone tools.
  - Constraint: a `Trace` serialises no angle (`libs/librepcb/core/geometry/trace.cpp:236`), the format is version 2 and stable, and the developer documentation accepts format changes on master only while the next major release is being worked on, a window of a few months every one to two years. Everything downstream of the file already handles arcs in a `Path` (DRC flattening at 5000 nm, Gerber G02/G03, `Path::arcObround`).
  - Horizon has arc tracks (start, end, centre), syncs them into its router copy as `PNS::ARC`, commits them back with a centre, and exposes all four corner modes in its router settings window. KiCad's own arc trouble: issue 9023 (shove collisions in arc corner mode, fixed for 6.0.8 by MR 1009, which touched kimath only), 15607 (walkaround slower with rounded corners, closed for 8.0), 9611 (free angle mode, open).
- Decision, engine first: the crate gets arcs in full (work item 012, eight slices), LibrePCB keeps its corner mode mitered and refuses the rounded ones at the settings boundary, as it already refuses the round meander style. The alternative of flattening rounded corners into chords on commit at the DRC tolerance needs no format change but bloats the file and loses the arc, so it is not taken; the alternative of a fork only format change is the same kind of change declined for the pair concept.
- Decision, representation: KiCad's three point form with no cached derived values, and two named lossy constructors for hosts that store centres or angles. Note 09 section 11.2 has the argument; the short form is that three integer points are the only representation that is exact and survives a round trip with KiCad's board file, and that caching a centre means deciding what to do when it goes stale, which KiCad does not (note 09 erratum E4).
- Measurement recorded for the fixtures: the corpus has one board with arc tracks (`stickhub-extra-via.kicad_pcb`, 180 arcs) and no case replays it. KiCad's regression runner turns an unresolved board hash into a pass, so at the reference commit six of its eleven cases run nothing (note 09 erratum E34). Arc behaviour above the geometry layer therefore rests on the port's own scenarios and recorded sessions.

## Erratum E1: the arc centre keeps KiCad's round number snapping

Tags: #decision #arcs #numerics

- Decision: `calc_arc_center` in `src/geometry/arc.rs` reproduces `libs/kimath/src/trigo.cpp:529` to `:550` verbatim, uncertainty propagation and all. After computing the circumcentre from the two perpendicular bisector slopes, KiCad replaces it with the nearest multiple of 100 nm, or failing that 10 nm, whenever the first order uncertainty it carried through every term covers that round value. Its comment at `:534` is the justification: "This is justified because ALL values within the uncertainty range are equally true."
- What that costs: the centre is not a continuous function of the three points. Moving one endpoint by a nanometre can move the reported centre by 50, and the radius, the sweep, the length and the polyline approximation all inherit the jump. Nothing else in the crate's numeric model behaves this way, and no amount of care in the Rust code removes it: it is inherent to deriving a centre from three integer points.
- Why verbatim anyway, the two concrete reasons. First, bit compatibility. Every arc centre the crate reports has to agree with KiCad's for a golden comparison against KiCad's corpus to mean anything, and the crate already leans on that footing for the regression replays. Dropping the snap moves every centre by up to 50 nm relative to KiCad's, which is not a difference a golden can absorb. Second, `TOPOLOGY::AssembleDiffPair` requires two arc centres to be within 5 nanometres (`pcbnew/router/pns_topology.cpp:1109`, note 09 erratum E9). That is a comparison between two independently snapped centres: with the snap, two arcs of the same physical curve land on the same round value and pass; without it, the two land on whatever the raw arithmetic gives and the 5 nm window decides on the last bits of a `double`. Reproducing the snap is what makes that test behave as it does in KiCad. Slice 5 or 7 will meet it for real.
- Scope of the decision: it applies to the three point routine only. The two point plus angle routine (`trigo.cpp:329`) has no snapping in KiCad and gets none here, so `ShapeArc::from_start_end_angle` places its centre by one rounding rule and then reports one placed by the other. That asymmetry is KiCad's and is documented on the constructor rather than smoothed over.
- Recorded while porting, not in note 09: the `VECTOR2D` to `VECTOR2I` conversion at `libs/kimath/include/math/vector2d.h:85` **truncates** towards zero after clamping, it does not round. `ConstructFromStartEndAngle` takes that path (`shape_arc.cpp:205`) while the three point centre takes `KiROUND` (`trigo.cpp:573`), so the two centre routines differ in their final rounding as well as in the snapping. Both are reproduced.
- Also recorded: `qa/tests/libs/kimath/geometry/test_shape_arc.cpp:1395` to `:1495` holds **seven** `CalcArcCenter` cases, not the six note 09 section 8.1 and work item 012 count. All seven are mirrored in `src/geometry/arc.rs`.

## Slice 1 of the arcs: what the port does differently from KiCad

Tags: #decision #arcs #numerics

- A third named constructor was added beyond the two the work item lists. `ShapeArc::from_center_start_angle` ports `SHAPE_ARC( center, start, centralAngle, width )` (`libs/kimath/src/geometry/shape_arc.cpp:41`) because two of the mirrored tests build their arcs with it (`test_shape_arc.cpp:1150` and `:1473`) and because `ROUNDED_45` needs it in slice 6 (`libs/kimath/src/geometry/direction_45.cpp:194`). It is lossy in the same way as the other two and says so.
- Float to integer narrowing saturates where C++ leaves it undefined. Rust's `as` cast saturates, so the three `double` to `int` narrowings inside `ConvertToPolyline` and the `CIRCLE` radius inside `NearestPoint` clamp instead of producing whatever the hardware does. The only arc that reaches the difference is one whose radius is at or beyond `INT_MAX / 2`, and `ConvertToPolyline` sends that one down the chord branch anyway.
- Three places widen to `i64` where KiCad stays in `int` and can wrap: the offset inside `rotate_point`, the quadrant points the bounding box merges, and every point difference an angle is taken of. KiCad widens the last of these itself in `GetStartAngle` and `GetCentralAngle` (`VECTOR2L`, `shape_arc.cpp:938`, `:989`) and not in `sliceContainsPoint` (`:1149`); the port widens in all of them. The results agree wherever KiCad does not wrap, which is everywhere inside the coordinate envelope.
- `ConvertToPolyline`'s `aActualError` out parameter is not ported. Nothing in `pcbnew/router/` reads it.
- `SHAPE_ARC::Mirror( const VECTOR2I&, FLIP_DIRECTION )` is not ported, only the `SEG` axis overload, matching what `LineChain::mirror` already does for the same reason: the reference point overload has no router caller.
- `euclidean_norm_f64` now lives in `src/geometry/math.rs` as a public function, because the arc centre needs the 45 degree and axis aligned special cases of `VECTOR2<double>::EuclideanNorm` on operands that never were coordinates. `src/geometry/vec2.rs` still has a private copy of the same three cases behind `Vec2::distance`. Collapsing the two is a tidy up for whoever next has reason to touch `vec2.rs`; this slice was not allowed to.

## Erratum E2: `Collide( SEG )` keeps reporting the last candidate that collided

Tags: #decision #arcs #numerics

- Decision: reproduced. `ShapeArc::collide_seg` in `src/geometry/arc.rs` walks KiCad's candidate list in KiCad's order and keeps the gap and location of the **last candidate that collided**, not the nearest one. The test naming it is `collide_seg_reports_the_last_candidate_erratum_e2`, so a later fix is a visible test change.
- Why not fixed. The behaviour is not undefined, is not a crash and is not unreachable, so the milestone rule says reproduce. Beyond the rule there is a concrete reason: `CollideSeg` (`test_shape_arc.cpp:799`) asserts the defective numbers. Its "seg inside arc start" row expects a gap of exactly the clearance, and its "large diameter arc" row expects 433268 against a clearance of 433300. Both are last candidate values. Fixing the routine would have meant editing the mirrored table, which is the one thing that makes the mirror worth having, and the ten rows now pass unchanged.
- What depends on the defect. `Collide( SHAPE_ARC, SHAPE_SEGMENT )` (`shape_collisions.cpp:763`) is the row the router reaches for an arc track against a straight track, and it is the only arc row that does not go through `NearestPoints`. Its gap feeds `ITEM::collideSimple`'s castellation and net tie tests and the shove's clearance arithmetic. An arc that is genuinely 10 nm from a segment can be reported as 400 nm away, so a fix would move real routing decisions, not just a number. That is slice 7's problem to meet with a scenario, not slice 2's to pre-empt.
- Recorded while porting, a correction to note 09's wording. The erratum text says the reported distance is "whichever candidate happened to be evaluated last, which is `aSeg.B`". It is the last candidate that **collided**: `Collide( const VECTOR2I& )` writes `*aActual` and `*aLocation` only on its true path (`shape_arc.cpp:922` to `:929`), so a candidate that misses leaves the previous one's numbers in place. `aSeg.B` is only the answer when `aSeg.B` itself collides.
- Also recorded: KiCad's loop returns at the **first** colliding candidate when the caller passed no `aActual` pointer (`:333`). The crate always computes the gap and the location together, so that early exit can only change which candidate's numbers are discarded, never the boolean. The port therefore always runs the full loop and says so on the method.

## Erratum E3: `NearestPoints( SHAPE_RECT )` loses the half width clamp, it does not ignore the width

Tags: #decision #arcs #numerics

- Decision: reproduced. `ShapeArc::nearest_points_to_rect` takes the minimum of the segment overload over the rectangle's four outline segments and then recomputes the squared distance from the two points, exactly as `shape_arc.cpp:643` and `:644` do. The test naming it is `nearest_points_to_rect_drops_the_width_clamp_erratum_e3`.
- Why not fixed. Same rule, and the same argument as E2: the behaviour is defined and consistent, and nothing in the router reaches the row (note 09 section 6), so there is no routing decision to protect. Fixing it would put the crate one silent behaviour away from KiCad on a row a host with `SHAPE_RECT` pads will eventually exercise, and the divergence would be invisible until that host appeared. Reproducing it keeps the divergence in a test name instead.
- Correction to note 09, found while porting and worth writing down because the erratum's own text is wrong about the mechanism. E3 says the rect overload "returns the raw centre line distance" and note 09 section 6's table says "width ignored". Neither is right. The overload delegates to `SHAPE::NearestPoints( const SHAPE* )` on the rectangle's outline (`shape_arc.cpp:642`), which lands in the `SH_LINE_CHAIN` against `SH_ARC` row of the nearest point dispatcher (`shape_nearest_points.cpp:658`), which calls the **`SEG` overload** per outline segment. That overload does pull the point on the arc in by half the arc width and does clamp the distance to zero when the half width covers the gap. What the rect overload then throws away is only the clamp: its last line recomputes `aDistSq` from the two points, one of which has already been moved.
- The measurable consequence, from the test. A quarter circle of radius 1 mm about the origin, 400 um wide, against a rectangle whose near side is 100 um from the arc's centre line. The segment overload answers zero, the two overlap. The rectangle overload answers 88 um of separation, because `Resize` moved the point on the arc 200 um towards a side only 100 um away and the recomputed distance measures the overshoot. With the width at zero the same query answers the true 100 um. So a wide arc against a rectangle is reported as further away the wider it gets, past the point where its edge crosses the rectangle.
- Another note 09 detail: section 1.7's table says the rect overload "delegates to `SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN::NearestPoints`". There is no such member. The entry point is `SHAPE::NearestPoints( const SHAPE*, VECTOR2I&, VECTOR2I& )` (`geometry/shape.h:258`), dispatched by `nearestPointsSingleShapes` (`shape_nearest_points.cpp:597`).

## Slice 2 of the arcs: what the port does differently from KiCad

Tags: #decision #arcs #numerics

- `Collide` measures the radius with a different expression from `GetRadius`, and the port keeps both. `update_values` fills `m_radius` with `sqrt( (VECTOR2D(m_start) - m_center).SquaredEuclideanNorm() )` (`shape_arc.cpp:414`), while both `Collide` overloads recompute it as `VECTOR2D( center - m_start ).EuclideanNorm()` (`:259`, `:858`). `EuclideanNorm` short circuits the exact diagonal and the two axis aligned cases before it reaches `hypot`, so the two expressions disagree in the last bit on some inputs. `ShapeArc::collide_radius` is the second one and is used only by the two collision routines, which is why it is private.
- The gap the `NearestPoints` based rows report is `KiROUND( sqrt( dist_sq ) )` (`shape_collisions.cpp:622`), a **rounding** square root. That is a deliberate exception to the rule in the `collision` module documentation, which says a truncating `(int) sqrt( dist_sq )` is replaced by the crate's exact integer square root. KiCad does not truncate here, so the rule does not apply and `kiround` is used instead. `CollideCircle` expects 53319 and would not get it from a truncating root.
- Widenings, in the same spirit as slice 1 and each noted at the call site: the clearance plus half width sum in `collide_point`, every point difference an angle or a norm is taken of, the squared distances from a segment endpoint to the centre in `collide_seg`, the two `Resize` inputs in the half width adjustments, and the rotated solution and the centre add back in the circle to circle intersection. KiCad computes all of them in `int` and can wrap.
- `CIRCLE::Intersect( const CIRCLE& )` and `CIRCLE::FurthestPoint` are ported into `src/geometry/arc.rs` next to the existing `circle_nearest_point`, because the two `nearest_points` overloads that need them are the only callers in the crate. `CIRCLE::NearestPoint( const VECTOR2D& )`, the floating point overload `Collide( const VECTOR2I& )` uses, is there too. `CIRCLE::IntersectLine` and `Intersect( const SEG& )` already lived in `src/geometry/collision.rs` and were made reachable from `arc` by taking a centre and a radius instead of the module private `CircleRef`.
- The four pairwise rows are free functions in `src/geometry/collision.rs`, `collide_arc_circle`, `collide_arc_rect`, `collide_arc_segment` and `collide_arc_arc`, with `_mtv` forms for the three rows KiCad can produce a translation vector for. Nothing dispatches to them: `Shape` gains its arc variant in slice 4. They are public rather than private because a private function only a test calls is dead code in a release build.
- Two rows are **not** in this slice and slice 4 has to pick them up. `Collide( SHAPE_ARC, SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN )` (`shape_collisions.cpp:636`) and `Collide( SHAPE_ARC, SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN_BASE )` (`:786`) both walk a chain, and the first needs `IsArcSegment` and `Arc( i )`, which arrive with slice 3. The consequence for slice 2 is that `collide_arc_rect` takes no corner radius: KiCad's rounded rectangle branch (`:723`) hands the whole thing to the line chain row, and there is nothing to hand it to yet.
- `CollideArc` (`test_shape_arc.cpp:948`) checks its fifteen rows four ways, arc against arc, arc against chain, chain against arc and chain against chain. Only the first is mirrored, for the same reason. The other three belong to slice 3 or 4, and the fifteen expectations are identical in all four, so the mirror loses no coverage of the collision answer itself, only of the chain plumbing.
- `IntersectLine`, `Intersect( CIRCLE )` and `Intersect( SHAPE_ARC )` on `SHAPE_ARC` are still not ported, as note 09 section 12 asks. `NearestPoints` needs none of them: the two overloads that intersect circles call `CIRCLE::Intersect` directly.
- What the mirrored tables prove. `CollidePt`'s 41 rows, `CollideSeg`'s 10 rows with their expected collision points, `CollideCircle` and `CollideArc`'s 15 rows all pass against the values KiCad asserts, with no tolerance anywhere. Every `f64` path in slice 1 and slice 2 therefore agrees with KiCad's to the nanometre on those inputs, which is the footing the arc goldens of slices 5 to 8 will stand on.

## Erratum decisions for slice 3 of the arcs

Tags: #decision #arcs

The milestone rule is reproduce faithfully behind a test named after the erratum, unless the behaviour is undefined, a crash or unreachable, in which case fix it and say why. Every test below lives in `src/geometry/line_chain.rs` and carries the erratum number in its name, so a later change to any of these decisions is a visible test change.

- **E5**, `Insert( size_t, const SHAPE_ARC&, int )` reads out of bounds (`shape_line_chain.cpp:1674`). **Fixed**, an out of bounds read being outside what the rule asks to reproduce. `LineChain::insert_arc` scans forward from the insertion point for the first arc the surviving vertices still refer to and splices there, which is the position chain order asks for at every vertex. The erratum's second half, that `Insert` skips `Append`'s "more than two points" demotion so a degenerate arc still gets an entry, is fixed too: both arc entry points now apply the same rule, because two entry points that disagree about what counts as an arc is a trap with no upside. Test: `insert_arc_finds_its_position_without_reading_past_the_end_erratum_e5`. Note that `Insert( SHAPE_ARC )` has no router caller (note 09 section 2.7); it is ported because E5 needs something to be a decision about, and because the fixed form is four lines shorter than the broken one.
- **E6**, `Remove`'s arc dropping iterates its index set in increasing order while `convertArc` renumbers every index above the one it erased (`:1118` to `:1157`). **Reproduced.** The removed point range is contiguous, so the doomed arc indices are contiguous too, and the net effect is that every other arc of the range is erased while the ones between it are left behind with every reference to them gone. That is defined behaviour, not a crash, and it is observable as an arc count that drops by half of what a caller would expect. Test: `remove_range_erases_every_other_arc_of_the_range_erratum_e6`. What keeps it harmless here is E13's fix: the arcs left behind are orphans and `live_arcs` never yields them, so they cost memory and nothing else.
- **E7**, `Replace( int, int, const SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN& )` appends the incoming arcs at the end of `m_arcs` whatever position their points took (`:1071`). **Fixed**, as note 09 section 11.1 change 2 already decided. `replace_with_chain` splices them into position, and a private `check_invariants` asserts chain order after every mutator in a debug build. The reason this one cannot be reproduced is that `reverse` remaps index `i` to `len - i - 1`, which is a reversal only while the arcs are in chain order, so reproducing E7 would make reversing twice stop being the identity and the slice's own exit criterion would fail. Tests: `replace_with_chain_splices_arcs_into_chain_order_erratum_e7` and the property test `reversing_twice_is_the_identity`.
- **E10**, `Slice` copies points forward from a start inside an arc with no end bound (`:1444`), so a range whose two ends are interior to one arc runs on to that arc's end. **Reproduced.** Defined, and `LINE::restoreUntouchedArcs` reaches it (`pns_line.cpp:285`), so slice 7 will meet it with a scenario rather than being surprised by it. Test: `slice_inside_one_arc_runs_past_its_end_erratum_e10`.
- **E12**, `Simplify2`'s colinear run checks the shape entries of the run's first two vertices and then never looks again (`:2968` to `:2974`). **Reproduced.** Defined, and reachable from the walkaround, `LINE::Walkaround` and the shove, all three of which the optimizer's convergence depends on; changing what `Simplify2` drops is exactly the kind of change note 01 section 6.5 warns moves optimizer behaviour. Test: `simplify2_can_drop_an_arcs_interior_points_erratum_e12`, which stores an arc of three nanometres of sagitta at an accuracy of one so that its approximation points sit inside the one nanometre tolerance.
- **E13**, orphaned arcs stay in `m_arcs` and still collide and still draw (`:480`, `:873`, `router_preview_item.cpp:278`). **Fixed by construction**, note 09 section 11.1 change 3. The arc vector is private and the only way to reach it is `live_arcs`, which walks the shape entries and yields each referenced arc once in chain order. `collide_point`, `collide_seg` and `length` all go through it. Test: `an_orphaned_arc_neither_collides_nor_adds_length_erratum_e13`, which builds the orphan through E6 and then checks that it neither collides nor adds length.
- **E14**, `NearestPoint`'s arc snapping can advance past the last vertex and then read the unchecked `ArcIndex` and `Arc` (`:2427` to `:2450`). **Fixed** by change 4, both accessors returning `Option`; the snap falls back to the unsnapped nearest point. Test: `nearest_point_snapping_stays_in_range_erratum_e14`, on the only shape that reaches it, a closed chain whose last segment is an arc segment.
- **E35**, `IsArcEnd( 0 )` looks back at the last vertex whether the chain is closed or not (`:3286`). **Moot**, as note 09 says, and the port keeps KiCad's unconditional look back because it cannot change an answer. Recorded while porting, because the erratum's own text is wrong about the consequence: it says the first point "can therefore be reported as an arc end because the **last** segment is an arc segment", and that cannot happen. The look back asks `IsArcSegment` of the last vertex, and on an open chain `nextIdx` is then `m_shapes.size()`, the closed guard fails and the predicate returns false, so `IsArcEnd( 0 )` is simply always false on an open chain. Test: `is_arc_end_at_vertex_zero_never_wraps_erratum_e35`.

## Slice 3 of the arcs: what the port does differently from KiCad

Tags: #decision #arcs

- `ArcRef` carries the vertex's role, so `is_arc_start`, `is_arc_end` and `is_pt_on_arc` are field reads instead of point comparisons against an arc that `amendArc` and `Slice`'s re-cut can rebuild. Every mutator that cuts or renumbers a run of vertices has to restate the roles, which is what the private `mark_arc_run_roles` is for. That is the cost of change 1 and it is worth naming: KiCad recomputes the roles from geometry every time and so cannot get them out of step, while this port can and relies on `check_invariants` to catch it.
- Correction to note 09 section 11.1, found while porting. The section writes `is_arc_segment` as `matches!(shapes[i], Plain) == false && shapes[i].leaving_arc() == shapes[i+1].entering_arc()`. That predicate is wrong: at an arc's last point followed by a plain vertex, `leaving_arc` is absent by the section's own rule and `entering_arc` is absent too, the two compare equal and a straight segment reads as an arc segment. The comparison has to require an arc on **both** sides, which is what the port does.
- Second correction to the same section. It says the role "removes the need for the predicate to be geometric at all" and that `is_arc_segment` needs "no wrap special case". The first is true, the second is not: the role says what the next vertex holds but not **which** vertex is next, and on a closed chain the segment leaving the last vertex arrives at vertex zero. KiCad's guard, `aSegment + 1 == m_shapes.size() && m_closed && IsSharedPt( 0 )` (`shape_line_chain.cpp:3257`), is kept verbatim for that reason.
- Recorded while porting, not in note 09 and not an erratum of its own, though it is the same class of defect as E6. `SetPoint` runs `convertArc` over both halves of the vertex's shape entry through `alg::run_on_pair` (`:1371`), which applies the lambda to `pair.first` and then to `pair.second`, reading the live pair. `convertArc` rewrites that very entry: it clears the half that named the erased arc and swaps, so by the time the second call runs the entry's second half is already the sentinel and the call does nothing. At a shared vertex only the arc **ending** there is destroyed, and the arc starting there survives with a start point that no longer matches the moved vertex. Reproduced, the behaviour being defined, and `check_invariants` deliberately does not assert that an arc's stored endpoints equal the chain points at the ends of its run, because this is one of two places where they legitimately disagree.
- Recorded while porting, a reading of `test_shape_line_chain.cpp` that the mirrored tests depend on. `SHAPE_ARC arc( VECTOR2I( 200000, 0 ), VECTOR2I( 300000, 0 ), ANGLE_180 )` in the `Split`, `Slice` and `NearestPointPt` cases is not the start, end, angle form: it is the **centre, start, angle** constructor (`shape_arc.h:57`), which is the only three argument one. Reading it the other way gives an arc of half the radius, six polyline points instead of seven, and every point count in those three cases off by one. Note 09 section 8.2 does not say which constructor the cases use and a port will guess wrong.
- `LineChain::slice` needed a second form. KiCad has `Slice( int, int )` and `Slice( int, int, int aMaxError )` (`:1412`, `:1417`), and the accuracy matters because a whole arc that fits inside the range is **re-polygonised** at it. The two argument form keeps its signature and passes the new `ARC_POLYGONIZATION_MAX_ERROR`, 1000 nm, which is KiCad's default; `slice_with_max_error` is the three argument one. Without it the mirrored `Slice` case, which passes `ARC_HIGH_DEF`, cannot be written.
- Note 09 section 11.3 lists `split_three_way` among the members that are deliberately polyline only. That is true of its own body and of nothing else: KiCad's five argument `Split` calls `NearestPoint` with `aAllowInternalShapePoints` cleared, then `Split`, `Reverse` and three `Slice`s (`:2877` to `:2902`), every one of which is arc aware. The port inherits the same behaviour and its doc comment says so rather than claiming a blindness it does not have.
- `nearest_point` grew KiCad's `aAllowInternalShapePoints`. The crate's three call sites all transcribe KiCad sites that take the default `true`: `best_anchor_for_point` in `src/dragger.rs` (`pns_dragger.cpp:641`), the hull snap in `src/placer/line_placer.rs` (`pns_line_placer.cpp:830`) and the hull walk in `src/shove.rs` (`pns_shove.cpp:359`, which passes `true` explicitly). `split_three_way` passes `false`, as `shape_line_chain.cpp:2882` does. `pns_helpers.cpp:98`, the one KiCad site that passes `false` from the router, has no counterpart in the crate yet.
- `ClearArcs` is ported even though note 09 section 12 lists it among the members with no router caller. The work item's slice 3 asks for it, it is four lines, and it is the only operation that degrades a whole chain's arcs at once, which the host boundary of slice 8 will want.
- The arc vector is not exposed. There is no `arcs()` accessor to match KiCad's `CArcs()`, only `arc_count`, `arc( i )` and `live_arcs`, because an accessor returning the whole vector is exactly the escape hatch E13 came through.
- Cost of the two new fields, recorded because it is a real one. `LineChain` grew from 40 to 88 bytes and every chain now carries a second heap allocation as long as its point vector, whether or not it holds an arc. That is KiCad's data model and the port follows it, but it means an arc free chain is measurably more expensive than it was. Two enums outside `src/geometry/` crossed clippy's `large_enum_variant` threshold as a result, `LineVia` (`src/line.rs:169`) and `FixOutcome` (`src/router.rs:853`); both got an `#[allow]` with a comment rather than a `Box`, because boxing would move a hot path's payload to the heap to satisfy a lint about a value built once per call.
- What the mirrored tests prove. ## Erratum decisions for slice 4 of the arcs

Tags: #decision #arcs

- **E21**, `ArcHull` dereferences the two `IntersectLines` optionals without checking (`pns_utils.cpp:131`, `:132`). **Fixed**, an unchecked dereference of an empty optional being undefined rather than defined behaviour. `arc_hull` returns `Result<LineChain, ArcHullError>` and answers `ArcHullError::CollinearMitre { segment }` there. What the caller gets: `build_hull_for_primitive_shape` maps every error to `None`, which is the same answer it already gives for the shapes it cannot hull, so the item model's compound mapping needs no new case. Could KiCad reach it? No, and the test says so rather than asserting note 09's claim on trust: `arc_hull_answers_an_error_instead_of_reading_past_the_end_erratum_e21` builds 28 arcs across four radii from 0.1 mm to 100 mm and seven sweeps from half a degree to 359.5 and checks every one comes back `Ok`. `ConvertToPolyline` only adds a vertex once the sagitta is worth one, so at `ARC_LOW_DEF` consecutive chords always turn. The guard is for a caller that lowers the accuracy, which the polygonisation argument invites.
- **E21, second half.** The same routine reads `line.Segment( 0 )` without checking that the polyline has a segment (`:94`). Fixed the same way, `ArcHullError::DegenerateArc`. Reaching it takes an arc whose three points coincide **and** a combined clearance of zero, because a chord of zero otherwise satisfies the `chord < cl` test of the whole circle branch at `:76` and that branch answers an octagon around a point. The test passes `clearance` and `walkaround_thickness` of zero for exactly that reason and says so.
- **E38**, `ArcHull` and `SegmentHull` round the same two quantities differently. **Reproduced verbatim**, as the work item asks, because the walkaround's termination depends on hulls and the collision predicates agreeing to the nanometre (note 01 section 14.6). Both halves are in `arc_hull`: the half width of the moving line is `( t + 1 ) / 2` (`:73`) where `segment_hull` takes `t / 2` (`:186`), and the corner cut is `(int)( 2.0 / ( 1.0 + M_SQRT2 ) * d ) / 2` (`:86`) where `segment_hull` takes `KiROUND( x / 2.0 )` (`:190`). The test naming it is `arc_hull_and_segment_hull_round_the_octagon_differently_erratum_e38`, and it asserts behaviour rather than its own arithmetic: an odd thickness gives the arc the hull of the next even thickness up and the capsule the hull of the next even thickness down, and at a common offset of 355000 nm the two builders emit the same eight vertices, four apart in the cycle, one nanometre apart on the corner cut coordinates. Note 01 section 12.2 has the older reading of the half width, `t / 2` for both; `:73` is what the source says.

## Slice 4 of the arcs: what the port does differently from KiCad

Tags: #decision #arcs

- **A defect note 09 does not list, reproduced.** The whole circle branch of `ArcHull` (`pns_utils.cpp:76` to `:83`) builds `OctagonalHull` around the arc's **centre line** circle inflated by `cl`, where the mitred branch offsets by `width / 2 + cl + DefaultAccuracyForPCB` (`:85`). The arc's own half width is therefore missing from the hull, which is half a track short on the outside and cuts inside the copper once the width passes twice the clearance. Doubling the width of such an arc changes the hull not at all. Reproduced, the behaviour being defined and the branch being reachable only for an arc that sweeps more than half a turn through an opening no line fits through, which the router will not route past anyway. Test: `arc_hull_of_a_whole_turn_leaves_the_arc_width_out`. It is worth a second look in slice 6, where the placer starts producing arcs.
- **The polyline against polyline row became arc aware, which slice 2 said it never would.** `Collide( SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN_BASE, SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN_BASE )` (`shape_collisions.cpp:351`) skips the segments that lie on a stored arc (`:378`, `:387`) and then collides the arcs whole in a rescue block (`:427`). Slice 2's note that this was "likewise dead here" stopped being true the moment slice 3 let a chain carry an arc, and it is what makes the chain against chain quarter of `CollideArc` give the same fifteen answers as the other three. Nothing changes for an arc free chain: `live_arcs` yields nothing and `is_arc_segment` is false everywhere.
- **`ChainRef`, a type the dispatch did not need before.** KiCad's arc handling in that row is gated on `aA.Type() != SH_LINE_CHAIN` (`:378`), and `SHAPE_SIMPLE` holds a `SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN` without being one, so a polygon contributes every segment including its arc segments and its stored arcs never reach the rescue block, whose `dynamic_cast` yields null (`:430`). The crate hands both cells a bare `&LineChain` and cannot tell them apart, so the polyline cells now take a `ChainRef` carrying that one bit. `rect_chain` and `rect_rect` take it too, because the rounded rectangle branches feed an outline into the same cell.
- **The two chain rows walk `live_arcs`, not the raw arc vector.** `Collide( SHAPE_ARC, SHAPE_LINE_CHAIN )` (`:636`) indexes `ArcCount` and `Arc( i )`, and so does the rescue block. Both go through `live_arcs` here, so an arc no vertex refers to any more cannot collide. That is erratum E13, fixed by construction in slice 3, and this is the second place it shows.
- **Where a translation vector request reaches an inner call, it arrives as the bare boolean one.** KiCad's chain rows pass `aActual` and `aLocation` straight down and never an `aMTV` (`:659`, `:683`, `:448`), so a caller who asked only for a vector reaches them with both pointers null. The port maps `Request::Mtv` to `Request::Boolean` at those three hand offs rather than letting the inner cell compute a vector nothing will read.
- **`collide_arc_rect` grew the corner radius argument.** Slice 2 left it out because the rounded branch had nowhere to go. It now takes `radius` and covers the whole of `:721`, so the free function is a complete port of the row rather than of one of its two branches.
- **The arc rows the dispatch gained.** Eleven arms: the six `SH_ARC` rows of `:1241` to `:1257` and the five mirrored ones at `:1090`, `:1123`, `:1156`, `:1189` and `:1223`, each reproducing `CollCaseReversed`'s negation (`:906`). Only the circle, rectangle and arc rows can produce a vector; the other three answer `Vec2::new(0, 0)`, which is this module's "no vector available", the same answer every pair KiCad asserts the request away for already gives. KiCad's arc rows are sign consistent with the module's convention, unlike the circle against polyline one, so no extra negation was needed anywhere.
- **`CollideArcToPolygonApproximation` is the one case of the slice that is not a literal mirror.** KiCad builds the obstacle out of `SHAPE_POLY_SET`, `TransformArcToPolygon` and a cached triangulation; the crate has none of the three and the router's world model contains no polygon set (note 01 section 11). The mirror builds the same buffer outline directly, at the same accuracy and with the same two error locations, and collides the arc against it at the same two clearances. The outline is kept **open**, because a closed one would swallow the arc's start point through the containment shortcut at `:650`, where KiCad's zone fill holds the buffer as a hole and so lies entirely outside it. The substitution is tight rather than approximate: the test also pins that the boundary sits at exactly the clearance from the arc's copper edge, so the two assertions straddle the answer by the epsilon and not by more.
- **Three match arms outside `src/geometry/`.** `src/diff_pair.rs:2222` joins `Shape::LineChain` in answering `(0, 0)`, which is what KiCad's `default:` does after its `wxFAIL_MSG` (`pns_diff_pair.cpp:499`). `src/eventlog.rs` gains an `arc` token in both directions, KiCad's log form `{ start, mid, end, width }` (`pns_logger.cpp:245`, which does have an `SH_ARC` case); a chain that carries arcs still loses them, because `chain_text` writes points only, and nothing in the world model can build one until slice 5. `tests/geometry_props.rs` samples an arc's three defining points, which are on the curve by construction; nothing in that file builds an arc yet.
- **Where the port can differ from KiCad by a nanometre, and why it does not here.** Every `f64` path in `arc_hull` is KiCad's own: the radius truncation at `:78`, the chamfer truncation at `:82`, the corner cut truncation at `:86`. The three `Resize` calls go through `Vec2::resize`, which is already the port of `VECTOR2::Resize` with its exact diagonal and axis cases. The one place the port widens is `octagonal_hull`'s vertex arithmetic, which panics in a debug build where KiCad wraps; a radius near `i32::MAX` reaches it through the `2 * r` of the circle branch, and no board can produce one. The collision rows add nothing new: they are built out of slice 2's primitives, whose mirrored tables already agree with KiCad to the nanometre.
- **What the mirrored tests prove.** `CollideArc`'s fifteen rows now pass in all four ways KiCad checks them, arc against arc, arc against chain, chain against arc and chain against chain, against the same expectations. `CollideArcToShapeLineChain` passes in both operand orders plus its bare segment. The hull table is the port's own, KiCad having no test for `ArcHull` at all (note 09 section 8.4 item 6): five arcs at two clearances, each hull clockwise and each holding a disc of `width / 2 + clearance` around every point of the arc's own `ARC_LOW_DEF` polyline, plus the exact eight vertices of the truncated corner cut for an offset where truncating and rounding disagree.
